Path of Light Through the Eye

Trace light rays as they enter the eye, refract through the cornea and lens, and focus on the retina. Compare normal vision, myopia, hyperopia, accommodation and corrective lenses.

Biology Human eye Light Refraction Vision

Learning Intention

Students will understand how the eye uses refraction to focus light onto the retina.

Success Criteria

Students can describe the role of the cornea, pupil, lens and retina, and explain how myopia, hyperopia and corrective lenses affect the focal point.

Student challenge

  • Set the eye to normal vision. Where do the rays focus?
  • Move the object closer without changing lens power. What happens to the focus?
  • Use accommodation to bring a near object back into focus.
  • Set the eye to myopia. Does the focus form before or behind the retina?
  • Set the eye to hyperopia. Does the focus form before or behind the retina?
  • Change pupil size. What happens to the width and number of rays entering the eye?

Teacher notes

This sim links the biology of the eye with the physics of refraction. Students should focus on the location of the focal point relative to the retina.

  • Good prompt: What does a correction lens need to do to light before it enters the eye?
  • Good prompt: Why does myopia need a concave lens?
  • Good prompt: Why does hyperopia need a convex lens?
  • Extension: Compare correction lenses with accommodation by the eye’s own lens.

Ideas used in the model

The model uses ray diagrams and relative lens power to show focusing.

Core idea: light changes direction when it refracts Sharp image: rays focus on the retina Object distance: far object + normal lens = focus on retina near object + unchanged lens = focus tends to shift behind the retina near object + stronger lens = focus returns to the retina Myopia / short-sightedness: rays focus before the retina a concave lens spreads rays slightly before they enter the eye Hyperopia / long-sightedness: rays focus behind the retina a convex lens brings rays together before they enter the eye Accommodation: changing lens shape changes lens power stronger lens power moves the focal point closer weaker lens power moves the focal point further away Pupil size: larger pupil = wider ray bundle smaller pupil = narrower ray bundle

How to explain it to students

  • Cornea: does much of the bending of incoming light.
  • Pupil: controls how much light enters the eye.
  • Lens: fine-tunes the focus by changing shape.
  • Retina: contains cells that detect light and start nerve signals.
  • Myopia: the focal point is too far forward.
  • Hyperopia: the focal point is too far back.
