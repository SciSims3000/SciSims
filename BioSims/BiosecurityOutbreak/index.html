How to use

  • Choose an outbreak scenario, then respond week by week.
  • Monitoring and research improve evidence confidence, but they cost money and take time.
  • Quarantine and treatment slow spread, but can affect farms, trade, movement and public support.
  • Culling/removal can be effective but may cause ecological, ethical or social consequences if overused.
Challenge: Finish with the outbreak contained, confidence above 70%, public trust above 55%, and economic and ecological impacts below severe.

What the model shows

Biosecurity decisions involve uncertainty. Early evidence may be incomplete, so managers need to balance fast action with better information. A delayed response may allow spread, while an excessive response can create unnecessary economic, ecological and social costs.

Key idea: Biosecurity is a systems problem. A good response uses evidence, acts proportionately, communicates clearly and adapts as new data arrives.

Teacher notes

  • Suitable for Stage 4/5 discussions of ecosystems, agriculture, disease, evidence, uncertainty and decision-making.
  • The model is conceptual. Values are classroom model units, not real outbreak data.
  • Suggested task: students export CSV data and write a CER response justifying their response strategy.
  • Extension: use Save run for comparison to compare an evidence-first response, a strong-containment response and an economy-first response under the same setup.
