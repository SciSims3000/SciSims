Sound Through the Ear

Follow a sound wave as it is collected by the outer ear, vibrates the eardrum, moves the ossicles, stimulates the cochlea and becomes nerve signals travelling to the brain.

Biology Sound Hearing Human body Waves

Learning Intention

Students will understand the pathway sound takes through the ear and how vibrations are converted into nerve signals.

Success Criteria

Students can identify the outer ear, ear canal, eardrum, ossicles, cochlea and auditory nerve, and describe their roles in hearing and noise damage.

Teacher notes

This sim links wave behaviour to human hearing. It also introduces noise-induced hearing loss as a health-literacy extension without presenting the model as a real decibel meter.

  • Good prompt: Does the sound wave itself travel to the brain?
  • Good prompt: Which parts vibrate mechanically?
  • Good prompt: Why does exposure time matter as well as loudness?
  • Extension: Compare hearing with seeing as sensory receptors converting stimuli into nerve signals.

Ideas used in the model

The sim uses wave properties to show how different sounds affect the ear.

Sound wave: frequency controls pitch amplitude controls loudness Frequency in the model: higher frequency = faster vibration lower frequency = slower vibration Sound pathway: outer ear collects sound ear canal guides sound eardrum vibrates ossicles transfer and amplify vibration cochlea detects vibration auditory nerve carries signals Damage-risk model: risk increases with loudness risk increases with exposure time hearing protection reduces the model dose damage is shown at the cochlear hair cells Important idea: The brain does not receive the sound wave directly. The ear converts vibration into nerve impulses.

How to explain it to students

  • Outer ear: collects and funnels sound.
  • Ear canal: carries the sound wave to the eardrum.
  • Eardrum: vibrates when pressure waves reach it.
  • Ossicles: small bones that transfer vibration to the cochlea.
  • Cochlea: detects vibration using hair cells.
  • Auditory nerve: carries information to the brain.
  • Damage point: loud sounds can damage cochlear hair cells, so the model places damage there.
