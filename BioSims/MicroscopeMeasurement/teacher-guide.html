Microscope Measurement Lab

Calibrate your field of view. Measure a specimen. Discover why a bigger image does not always reveal more detail.

Suggested time: 20–30 minutes. Use a projected demonstration, pairs or individual investigation.

Quick start

  1. Choose a specimen and objective. Set focus to zero; compare how the field diameter changes at 4×, 10× and 40× objectives.
  2. Line up the gold calipers with the horizontal edges of the central specimen. Alternatively, estimate how many specimens fit across the field.
  3. Enter a size estimate in mm or µm, then select Check & record. Repeat at another objective.
  4. In the separate detail test, change NA, point separation and digital enlargement one at a time. Record which changes affect the resolution criterion.

Discussion and expected reasoning

Why should the estimated specimen size remain similar when the objective changes?

Changing magnification changes the image and field, not the specimen’s physical size. Measurements may differ because of estimation uncertainty.

A field is 1.8 mm across. About ten specimens fit across it. What is one specimen’s estimated length in µm?

1.8 ÷ 10 = 0.18 mm = 180 µm.

Can 4× digital enlargement resolve detail that the objective could not resolve?

No. Digital enlargement makes existing information larger. In this model, resolution depends on wavelength and numerical aperture.

Worked example: estimate a plant cell

At a 10× objective, the 18 mm field number gives a field diameter of 1.8 mm = 1800 µm. If about ten central-cell widths fit across that diameter, estimate 180 µm = 0.18 mm. Enter either value with its matching unit, then check. Compare the estimate with the caliper measurement; explain any difference rather than treating the model answer as an observation.

Troubleshooting

If the specimen looks too small, select the 40× objective. Set focus offset to zero. Measure the horizontal width of the central specimen, using the gold calipers rather than a screen ruler. A larger eyepiece image does not change the field diameter in this model.

Check understanding

Ask students to explain why 0.18 mm and 180 µm describe the same length, then explain why digital enlargement cannot resolve two initially unresolved points.

Support and extension

Model one trial and ask students to make a prediction before operating the controls. Give students a results-table heading or sentence starter if needed. For extension, ask them to justify their method, evaluate an assumption and design a follow-up investigation.

Assess the explanation and evidence, not just completion of the activity.

Model limits

Specimens are schematic and have fixed dimensions, unlike biological variation in real specimens. The field number is fixed at 18 mm for both eyepieces. Screen dimensions are not a physical scale. The detail test uses a Rayleigh approximation and illustrative blur, not a full diffraction calculation. NA above 1 requires immersion.
