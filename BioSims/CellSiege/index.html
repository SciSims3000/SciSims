Cell Siege

A Space Invaders-style immune defence game where students control a white blood cell, fire immune responses and stop pathogens from overwhelming the body.

Learning Intention

Students will understand that different immune responses are suited to different biological threats, including pathogens, toxins and infected cells.

Success Criteria

Students can compare immune-response types, collect modelled wave data and explain how antibodies, phagocytes, killer T cells, barriers and immune memory affect infection control.

Immune Controls

Use the keyboard or on-screen controls. Compare one variable at a time.

Free play

Change any variable, start a wave and use the data to explain what happened.

Sound starts muted for classroom use.

Student Challenge

  1. Use Mission 1 to test how barrier strength changes body damage.
  2. Use Mission 2 to compare Macrophage and Antibody responses against bacteria.
  3. Use Mission 3 to test whether antibodies work better with immune memory.
  4. Use Mission 4 to explain why Killer T cells are useful against infected cells.
  5. Evaluate the model. What does this game show well, and what does it oversimplify?

Teacher Notes

  • This is a game-based model of immune defence, not a realistic cellular simulation.
  • The key concept is that immune responses have different roles and are more or less suited to different threats.
  • Useful terms: pathogen, antigen, antibody, phagocytosis, white blood cell, immune memory, vaccine, inflammation, infected cell and barrier defence.
