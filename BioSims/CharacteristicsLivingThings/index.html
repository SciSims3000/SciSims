Classification of living things

7.3.1 Characteristics of Living Things

Use MRS GREN evidence to decide whether an example is living, once-living or from a living thing, or non-living.

Set up the challenge

MRS GREN is a memory tool for the shared characteristics of living things: movement, respiration, sensitivity, growth, reproduction, excretion and nutrition.

Evidence tiles

Click the life-process evidence that is directly supported by the case file.

How to use:
Read the case file, choose the MRS GREN evidence that is directly supported, then classify the example. Check your answer and use the evidence notes to improve your reasoning.
Teaching point:
Living things carry out life processes. Some examples may only show some evidence in the case file, but the classification should still be based on whether they are organisms.

Lesson frame

Learning Intention

Students will learn about the characteristics shared by living things. They will understand how these characteristics can be used to decide whether something is living, once-living or non-living.

Success Criteria

Students can describe the main characteristics of living things, explain what MRS GREN stands for, and use evidence to classify examples as living, once-living or from a living thing, or non-living.

Teacher notes
  • The evidence tiles are based on what is directly stated in each case file. A living organism may carry out all life processes across its life cycle, even if only some are visible in a short case description.
  • Use the robot dog and fire examples to challenge the misconception that movement or apparent growth alone makes something living.
  • In easy mode, once-living or from a living thing examples are grouped with non-living examples as “not currently living”.
  • In full mode, students must separate objects that came from organisms from objects that were never organisms.
