Why classify?
Scientists classify living things so they can organise biodiversity, compare organisms, identify patterns and communicate clearly about the same group of organisms.

Lesson frame

Learning Intention

Students will learn about why scientists classify living things. They will understand how classification helps organise the huge diversity of life on Earth into useful groups.

Success Criteria

Students can explain why classification is important, describe how organisms are grouped using shared characteristics, and identify examples of major groups such as animals, plants, fungi, protists and bacteria.

Teacher notes
  • This sim uses broad classroom-level groups: animals, plants, fungi, protists and bacteria. It is designed for Year 7 pattern recognition rather than advanced taxonomy.
  • Emphasise that classification systems can change as scientists gather new evidence, especially genetic evidence.
  • Useful discussion question: “Which evidence was most important for separating protists from bacteria?”
  • Students may assume anything green is a plant. Use Euglena and cyanobacterium to challenge this.
  • Students may assume anything small is bacteria. Use yeast and amoeba to challenge this.
