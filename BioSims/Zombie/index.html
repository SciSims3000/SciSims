Zombie Outbreak Simulator

Use a fictional zombie outbreak to explore how contact rate, infection rate, response measures and safe zones can change the spread of a population-level outbreak.

Learning Intention

Students will understand how simple population models can represent spread, removal and control measures during an outbreak.

Success Criteria

Students can adjust model parameters, interpret H-Z-R population curves, and explain why small changes in spread or response can produce large differences over time.

Student challenge

  • Run the balanced outbreak. What happens to humans, zombies and removed over time?
  • Reset and increase the safe zone size. How does the curve change?
  • Increase the response/removal rate. What happens to the zombie peak?
  • Increase the infection rate. Does the outbreak grow faster or slower?
  • Export the CSV and identify the tick where the zombie population was highest.

Teacher notes

This is a fictional H-Z-R model inspired by compartment models. It is useful for discussing model assumptions, spread, control measures and why simulations are simplified representations of reality.

  • Good prompt: Which parameter has the biggest effect on the peak number of zombies?
  • Good prompt: What assumptions does this model make that would not be true in a real outbreak?
  • Extension: Compare this H-Z-R model with an SIR model: susceptible, infected and removed.

Formula used in the model

This sim uses a simple discrete-time compartment model. The population is grouped into three compartments: H humans, Z zombies/infected, and R removed. Each tick, the model estimates how many new infections occur and how many zombies are removed from active spread.

1

Work out how many humans are exposed

effectiveH = H × (1 − safezone)
2

Estimate contacts between humans and zombies

contacts = encounter × effectiveH × Z ÷ (effectiveH + Z)
3

Calculate new zombies

newZ = contacts × infection rate β
4

Calculate zombies removed from active spread

removed = response rate κ × Z + decay rate δ × remaining Z
5

Update the compartments

next H = H − newZ
next Z = Z + newZ − removed
next R = R + removed

How to explain it to students

The model asks three questions: how many risky contacts occur, how many contacts create new infections, and how many infected individuals are removed from active spread.

Encounter rate How often humans and zombies interact.
Infection rate β How likely a contact is to create a new zombie.
Response/removal rate κ How quickly zombies are removed from active spread.
Safe zone size The fraction of humans protected from contact.
Decay rate δ The rate at which zombies leave the active population without direct response.
Random outbreak spike A temporary increase in spread for one tick.

Important assumptions

  • The population is simplified into only three groups.
  • All exposed humans are treated as equally likely to meet zombies.
  • Safe zones reduce exposure rather than changing behaviour in detail.
  • The model is fictional and should be used to discuss patterns, not to predict real outbreaks.
