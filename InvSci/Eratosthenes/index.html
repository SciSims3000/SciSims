Eratosthenes Experiment Simulator

Use shadow angles and distance between two locations to estimate Earth’s circumference, just as Eratosthenes did using Alexandria and Syene.

Learning Intention

Students will understand that Earth’s circumference can be estimated by comparing shadow angles at two locations and applying proportional reasoning.

Success Criteria

Students can use distance and angle difference data to calculate Earth’s circumference, explain the geometry of the method, and evaluate limitations of the model.

Controls

NSW town mode uses a simplified latitude model. Shadow angle is approximated from latitude, as if measurements were taken at local solar noon near an equinox. It is useful for modelling, but it is not exact field data.
Circumference = 360 × distance ÷ angle difference
For best results, the two locations should be roughly north-south of each other and measurements should be taken at equivalent solar-noon conditions.

Student challenge

Use the NSW town model to choose two towns that are far apart north to south. Record the estimate, then add small amounts of measurement uncertainty. Explain why a small error in angle measurement can create a large error in the final circumference estimate.

Teacher notes

This simulation models the key geometry of Eratosthenes’ method. It assumes Earth is close to spherical, sunlight arrives in near-parallel rays, and location separation can be treated as north-south distance. In a real investigation, students could measure a stick height and shadow length at local solar noon, then calculate the shadow angle using trigonometry.

Assessing the simulation: Students should identify that the sim helps visualise proportional reasoning, but simplifies real-world issues such as longitude differences, uneven terrain, timing, atmospheric effects, seasonal solar declination, and uncertainty in distance measurement.
