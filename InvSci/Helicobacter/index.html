Marshall and Warren: Peptic Ulcer Evidence Simulator

Investigate how evidence changed the scientific explanation for many peptic ulcers, from stress and acid alone to a model involving Helicobacter pylori and other risk factors.

Learning Intention

Students will understand that scientific explanations can change when new evidence challenges an accepted model.

Success Criteria

Students can use evidence from simulated studies to compare competing explanations for peptic ulcers and evaluate limitations in the model.

Simulation evaluation prompt: Which variable in this simulation is most simplified? Explain how this model differs from a real clinical investigation.

Student challenge

Run three different investigation types. Use the evidence to decide whether the earlier stress and acid explanation is enough by itself. Then explain why a stronger scientific model can include more than one cause, such as H. pylori infection and NSAID use.

Teacher notes

This simulation is designed for modelling scientific investigation and changing explanations. It focuses on evidence, not diagnosis or treatment advice. Students should be encouraged to distinguish association, causation, treatment evidence, sample size, confounding variables and model limitations.

Historical context: Barry Marshall and Robin Warren were awarded the 2005 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for discovering Helicobacter pylori and its role in gastritis and peptic ulcer disease.

Sources: Nobel Prize summary, CDC H. pylori fact sheet, NIDDK peptic ulcer causes.

Assessing the simulation: Students should identify that the sim helps test how evidence changes explanations, but simplifies real clinical research by using modelled probabilities, simplified treatment categories, and a limited set of risk factors.
