Temperature and Reaction Rate Simulator

Investigate how temperature affects particle movement, successful collisions and reaction rate using a virtual chemical reaction.

Learning Intention

Students will understand that increasing temperature usually increases reaction rate because particles move faster and have more successful collisions.

Success Criteria

Students can manipulate temperature, collect reaction rate data, interpret a graph, and explain the trend using collision theory and model limitations.

Evidence and model

Current relationship

Increasing temperature increases particle speed, so particles collide more often and a larger fraction of collisions have enough energy to react.

Acid and metal question: Why does warming the acid usually make gas form faster?

Investigation steps

  1. Keep concentration and activation energy constant.
  2. Change temperature.
  3. Run and record each trial.
  4. Compare average reaction rates.

Controlled variables

Temperature investigation: keep concentration and activation energy constant so temperature is the independent variable.

Model explanation

The simulation uses a simplified collision model. Higher temperature makes particles move faster and increases the chance that a collision is successful.

Model limitation

This sim shows the pattern predicted by collision theory, but it does not calculate exact rates for a specific real chemical reaction.

Sensor precision is set to ±2%. Recorded product values include simulated measurement uncertainty.

Student challenge

  1. Use temperature investigation mode. Run at least five trials from cold to hot conditions.
  2. Identify the trend between temperature and average reaction rate.
  3. Explain the trend using particle speed, collision frequency and successful collisions.
  4. Change the activation energy or catalyst setting and describe how this changes the rate.
  5. Evaluate the simulation: what does it show well, what does it simplify, and how is it different from a real reaction experiment?

CER response

Teacher notes

  • This simulation supports reaction rate, collision theory, temperature effects, data collection, graphing and model evaluation.
  • The rate model is simplified and should not be treated as exact Arrhenius modelling.
  • The catalyst extension clearly separates the activation energy setting from the effective activation energy used by the model.
  • Safe practical links include magnesium and dilute acid, sodium thiosulfate and acid, enzyme reactions, or glow sticks in hot and cold water.
  • Students should assess the simulation by identifying strengths, limitations, differences from real investigations, possible sources of error, and an overall usefulness judgement.
  • Source base: OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Royal Society of Chemistry reaction rates resources, and Chemistry LibreTexts collision theory resources.
