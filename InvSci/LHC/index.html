Large Hadron Collider Model Simulator

Model how counter-rotating beams are steered by magnets, collide inside detectors, and produce particle tracks that can be filtered by a trigger system.

Learning Intention

Students will understand that the Large Hadron Collider uses high-energy beams, magnetic fields and large detectors to test models of matter and energy.

Success Criteria

Students can adjust a simplified LHC model, collect collision data, interpret event displays, and evaluate strengths and limitations of the simulation.

Evidence and model

Current model relationship

Two high-energy beams move in opposite directions. Magnetic fields steer the beams around the ring, and collisions occur where the beams cross inside detectors.

Model question: Why are detectors needed if the particles produced in a collision are too small to see directly?

Guided activity sequence

  1. Activity 1: change only beam energy.
  2. Activity 2: change only magnetic field.
  3. Activity 3: change only trigger selectivity.

Investigation steps

  1. Set beam energy and intensity.
  2. Adjust magnetic field strength.
  3. Generate collision events.
  4. Compare detected tracks and trigger selection.

Model explanation

The sim represents beams, magnets, collisions and detectors as simplified visual models. Higher energy events can create more energetic tracks, but not every event is recorded by the trigger.

Controlled variables

For a fair comparison, change one factor at a time, such as beam energy, field strength or trigger selectivity.

Model limitation

This is not a real particle physics simulation. It shows broad ideas about modelling, detection, selection and uncertainty.

Student challenge

  1. Activity 1: change only beam energy. Compare event energy and number of tracks.
  2. Activity 2: change only magnetic field. Explain what happens if the magnetic field is too weak for the selected beam energy.
  3. Activity 3: change only trigger selectivity. Explain why a detector system cannot record every possible event.
  4. Change detector focus and describe how the event display changes.
  5. Evaluate the simulation by identifying what it helps explain, what it simplifies, and how it differs from the real LHC.

CER response

Teacher notes

  • Suggested primary module: Investigating Science Module 4 or Module 6, depending on whether the focus is scientific modelling or technology.
  • This model supports scientific models, particle physics, data selection, detector technology, uncertainty and limitations of simulations.
  • The event display is symbolic. It does not use real event reconstruction or Standard Model calculations.
  • Useful teaching angle: the LHC does not “see” particles directly. It records detector signals and uses models to reconstruct events.
  • Students should assess the simulation itself by discussing strengths, limitations, differences from real experiments, possible sources of error, and overall usefulness.
  • Source base: CERN Large Hadron Collider and CERN experiment resources.
