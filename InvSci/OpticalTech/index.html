Learning Intention

Students will understand that scientific laws about light made it possible to design optical technologies that magnify, focus and transmit images beyond normal human vision.

Success Criteria

Students can link reflection and refraction laws to optical design, adjust design variables, collect evidence and assess the impact of these laws on microscopes and telescopes.

Student challenge

  • Use microscope mode. Reduce objective focal length and explain how magnification changes.
  • Use refracting telescope mode. Increase aperture and explain how brightness changes.
  • Use reflecting telescope mode. Compare the mirror with the lens in a refracting telescope.
  • Use optical fibre mode to explain how total internal reflection supports communication technology.
  • Reduce alignment and surface quality. Explain why accurate science is not enough if the technology is poorly made.
  • Assess how the laws of reflection and refraction contributed to microscopes and telescopes.

Teacher notes

This sim is best used for Investigating Science Module 6: Technologies, with a strong link to theories, laws and models. The central reasoning chain is: understand light behaviour → predict ray paths → design useful optical technology.

  • Good prompt: Which law or model is doing the design work?
  • Good prompt: What does the sim simplify compared with a real instrument?
  • Good prompt: How does technology turn a scientific law into a useful tool?
  • Law mismatch mode is intentional: students can see when a selected law does not strongly support the chosen technology.

Formula used in the model

The calculations are simplified estimates designed for classroom reasoning.

Microscope: magnification ≈ (tube length ÷ objective focal length) × (250 ÷ eyepiece focal length) Refracting telescope: magnification ≈ objective focal length ÷ eyepiece focal length Reflecting telescope: magnification ≈ primary mirror focal length ÷ eyepiece focal length Brightness model: brightness depends on aperture², magnification penalty, surface quality and alignment Image quality model: image quality depends on alignment, surface quality, brightness and design penalties Optical fibre: signal transmission depends on refractive index, alignment and surface quality

How to explain it to students

  • Refraction lets lenses bend rays predictably.
  • Reflection lets mirrors redirect and focus rays predictably.
  • Total internal reflection can trap light inside optical fibres.
  • Technology design uses laws and models to predict what will happen before the device is built.
  • Precision matters: poor alignment or poor surface quality can limit performance even when the science is correct.
