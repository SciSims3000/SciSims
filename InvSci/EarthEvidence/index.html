Earth History Evidence Builder

Build a scientific model of Earth’s geological history by adding evidence from technologies such as seafloor mapping, magnetic surveys, radiometric dating, GPS, computer reconstruction and seismic tomography.

Learning Intention

Students will understand that new technologies can increase the amount, quality and precision of evidence used to support scientific theories, laws and models.

Success Criteria

Students can identify evidence gained from different technologies, link evidence to Earth history models, and assess how technologies changed confidence in scientific explanations.

Evidence and model

Current model relationship

Technologies help scientists observe, measure and test Earth history models. As different evidence sources accumulate, confidence in a model can increase or the model may be refined.

Assessment question: Which technology had the greatest impact on the model, and why?

Best evidence for this model

Plate tectonics is strongest when seafloor mapping, magnetic striping, radiometric dating, GPS and tomography support the same explanation.

Computerised reconstruction view

Computer models are most powerful when they combine many evidence layers into one testable reconstruction of Earth history.

Investigation steps

  1. Select a scientific model focus.
  2. Add one technology at a time.
  3. Observe how the map, confidence meter and evidence table change.
  4. Assess which technology made the evidence stronger.

Model explanation

A computerised Earth history model becomes more useful when it combines multiple evidence sources, such as fossils, seafloor patterns, rock ages and present-day plate motion.

Controlled thinking

To assess impact clearly, change one technology layer at a time and describe what new evidence that technology contributes.

Model limitation

This simulation shows broad patterns. Real Earth history models use complex datasets, uncertainty estimates and specialist reconstruction software.

Student challenge

  1. Add surface geology and fossil evidence. Explain why this supports continental drift but may not be enough on its own.
  2. Add seafloor mapping, magnetic striping and radiometric dating. Explain how these technologies strengthened seafloor spreading and plate tectonic models.
  3. Add GPS and computer reconstruction. Explain how modern technology allows scientists to test and refine Earth history models.
  4. Choose one assessment lens, including awareness of limitations if appropriate, and identify which technology had the greatest impact.
  5. Evaluate the simulation: what does it help explain, what does it simplify, and how is it different from a real computerised geological model?

CER response

Teacher notes

  • Suggested primary module: Investigating Science Module 6, Technologies. Strong cross-module link to Module 4, Theories and Laws.
  • The focus is assessment of technological impact, not memorising plate tectonics content.
  • The confidence score is a teaching model. It is not a real statistical confidence calculation.
  • Useful examples include magnetic striping, ocean-floor age data, GPS vectors, GPlates-style reconstructions, palaeoclimate proxies and seismic tomography.
  • Students should assess the simulation itself by identifying strengths, limitations, differences from real modelling, sources of uncertainty and an overall usefulness judgement.
  • Source base: USGS plate tectonics resources, GPlates documentation, NASA palaeoclimate evidence resources, NOAA proxy data resources and EarthScope seismic tomography resources.
