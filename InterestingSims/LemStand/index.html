Lemonade Stand Lab

Run a virtual lemonade stand and investigate how price, stock, advertising, weather and random variation affect sales, profit and evidence-based decision-making.

Data Science Working Scientifically Variables Modelling

Learning Intention

Students will understand that models can be used to test decisions, identify relationships between variables and explain patterns in data.

Success Criteria

Students can adjust variables, collect repeated data, identify trends and use evidence to justify an improved business strategy.

Student challenge

  • Run at least 10 days using the same strategy. Record your total profit, mean profit and profit range.
  • Turn on Investigation Mode. Change only one independent variable, then run another 10 days.
  • Identify the independent variable, dependent variable and at least two controlled variables.
  • Use the graph and trial summary to justify your best lemonade stand strategy.
  • Explain why one very good or very bad day may not be enough evidence to prove a strategy works.

Assess the simulation

  • What did this simulation help you understand about variables and decision-making?
  • What are two strengths of this model?
  • What are two limitations of this model compared with a real lemonade stand?
  • How could random variation affect the reliability of your conclusions?
  • Overall, how useful is this simulation for learning about data and modelling? Give reasons.
Teacher notes

This simulation can be used to teach variables, repeated trials, model limitations, reliability, uncertainty and evidence-based decision-making. It is also suitable for introductory data science activities because students can export a CSV and analyse profit patterns in a spreadsheet.

Investigation Mode uses a repeatable pseudo-random sequence, so students can reset and compare different strategies under the same simulated weather and random variation. This supports fair testing and validity.
