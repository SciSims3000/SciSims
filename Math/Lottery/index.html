Lottery Probability Simulator

Choose numbers, run simulated lottery draws, and compare experimental outcomes with theoretical probability. This sim shows why rare events can feel possible but still remain extremely unlikely.

Maths Probability Combinations Experimental data

Learning Intention

Students will understand how probability can be estimated experimentally and calculated theoretically using combinations.

Success Criteria

Students can run repeated trials, compare experimental frequencies with theoretical probabilities, and explain why large samples give more stable patterns.

Student challenge

  • Use Quick pick, then run 100 draws. Which outcome appears most often?
  • Run 10,000 draws. Does the experimental pattern move closer to the theoretical pattern?
  • Compare the jackpot probability with the chance of getting no matches.
  • Switch to Powerball AU mode. Why does adding the Powerball make the jackpot less likely?
  • Export the CSV and calculate the experimental relative frequency for one outcome.

Teacher notes

For 6 from 45, the number of matching main numbers follows a hypergeometric model. For the simplified Powerball AU mode, main-number matching is combined with an independent Powerball match or non-match.

  • Good prompt: Why do experimental results fluctuate more with small samples?
  • Good prompt: Why is “possible” not the same as “likely”?
  • Extension: Ask students to explain the role of combinations in calculating theoretical probability.
