Sampling and Bias Lab

Sample a known virtual population. Compare selection methods, repeat your trials and separate consistent results from representative results.

Suggested time: 25–40 minutes. Use a projected demonstration, pairs or individual investigation.

Quick start

  1. Keep the population seed and distribution fixed. Start with the gradient population and n = 20.
  2. Take one sample, then Repeat 100 times. Change the method and repeat at the same sample size.
  3. Reveal the true population and compare the means, spreads and mean signed errors.
  4. Repeat with larger samples and the periodic population. Finish with n = 400 to check the census result.

Discussion and expected reasoning

Why does repeatedly taking the same convenience sample give zero spread?

The same units are selected every time. Identical estimates demonstrate repeatability, not representativeness.

What does a larger random sample usually change?

Across repetitions it usually reduces sampling variability. It does not guarantee an exact estimate from every individual sample.

Can systematic sampling be unreliable when the population has a repeating pattern?

Yes. The interval can align with the pattern, producing very different estimates from different random starting positions. This does not mean every systematic scheme is biased.

Support and extension

Model one trial and ask students to make a prediction before operating the controls. Give students a results-table heading or sentence starter if needed. For extension, ask them to justify their method, evaluate an assumption and design a follow-up investigation.

Assess the explanation and evidence, not just completion of the activity.

Model limits

400 equally sized units with a binary characteristic. Simple random sampling is without replacement; systematic sampling uses a random start and interval 400/n; convenience selects the first n units in row-major order near the path. No measurement error, non-response or missing data. SD is the spread of the recorded estimates, not a confidence interval. CSV exports reveal population truth even if hidden on screen.
