How to use the model

  1. Choose a furnace temperature.
  2. Press Run model and observe where vapours condense.
  3. Use Step 10 s to collect data without leaving the animation running.
  4. Explain why the light fractions collect higher in the column.

Science idea

Crude oil is a mixture of hydrocarbons. Fractions with lower boiling point ranges vaporise and then condense higher in the cooler part of the column. Higher-boiling fractions condense lower down. The heaviest residue remains near the bottom rather than travelling up the column as vapour.

Model limits

This is a classroom model. Boiling point ranges are approximate, collection rates are simplified, and the top of the column is held at 30 °C. Real refineries use controlled flow rates, pressure, trays, side streams and further processing such as cracking.
