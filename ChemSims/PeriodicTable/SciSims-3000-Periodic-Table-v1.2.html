First ionisation energy

Select a point, or focus it and press Enter, to highlight its element. Circles: evaluated values without NIST brackets; diamonds: theoretical values in parentheses; squares: estimates in square brackets. Missing or excluded values are not joined across gaps. The line only guides the eye between adjacent atomic numbers. Uncertainties appear in the selected-value readout and CSV; error bars are not drawn.

What does the graph measure?

Energy required to remove one electron from an isolated neutral gas-phase atom in its ground state: X(g) → X⁺(g) + e⁻. It often rises across a period and falls down a group, with important exceptions from subshell structure and electron pairing. Compare Be/B and N/O rather than treating the trend as a strict rule.

Source: NIST Atomic Spectra Database, neutral-atom extract checked 17 October 2026; 108 values, with no value supplied here for elements 109–118. Status flags follow NIST’s notation guide. Hydrogen’s very precise value is theoretical. 1 eV per atom = 96.48533212331002 kJ mol⁻¹, from the exact SI elementary charge and Avogadro constant. Uncertainties are retained as supplied; a blank is not zero. Displayed conversions are rounded for readability; CSV also preserves the original eV strings and references.

Read the science & conventions

States assume equilibrium at 1 atm (101.325 kPa), with no reaction or supercooling. These are states of the element, not its compounds. Phosphorus is represented by white phosphorus: it is liquid at 100 °C; red and black phosphorus remain solid. Carbon is graphite and arsenic is grey arsenic. Helium has no solid phase at 1 atm.

Astatine, francium and elements 100–118 are conservatively marked Unknown for bulk state at all three temperatures. This avoids presenting estimated transition temperatures as measured facts. Astatine's classification and the broad metal/non-metal classification of elements 109–118 are also marked Unknown. Their positions are established; family labels for elements 104–118 indicate expected periodic relationships, not measured bulk behaviour.

Atomic weights use the CIAAW 2024 abridged table, including stated uncertainties in element details. These are dimensionless relative quantities and can vary with isotopic composition. A value in square brackets is a selected isotope's integer mass number; it is not a standard atomic weight or an exact isotope mass. For elements without standard weights, isotope labels follow the IUPAC 2022 periodic table convention, with [97] for technetium as in NIST 2024.

Groups are numbered 1–18. The detached lanthanoid and actinoid rows retain periods 6 and 7. This 15-element detached-row layout assigns no individual group number to elements 57–71 or 89–103; the group-3 placeholders are not additional elements. The exact membership of group 3 depends on the convention used. Metalloid boundaries are not universally agreed; here B, Si, Ge, As, Sb and Te are metalloids and Po is a metal.

Shell diagrams describe neutral ground-state atoms of the first 20 elements only. Shell populations are 2,8,8,2 for calcium. The third shell can hold more than eight electrons in other elements; 2,8,8 is not a general shell-capacity rule. Rings represent energy levels schematically, not fixed electron paths or scale distances. Period gives occupied shell count for these atoms; groups 1–2 have 1–2 outer electrons and groups 13–18 have 3–8, except helium, whose first shell is full with two.

Discovery is a process: recognition, isolation, publication and formal validation may have different dates. Entries identify the relevant milestones and acknowledge disputed or independent work. Applications distinguish the element, its isotopes and its compounds.

Reference snapshot checked 17 October 2026. Each element links to its RSC reference page. Entries are original educational summaries; discovery milestones are qualified where source conventions differ.

The build used the public PubChem periodic dataset as an initial identity index. Weights, historical milestones and state handling were then checked and curated against the references above. No external code, fonts or data are loaded. Reference links need an internet connection.

How to use SciSims

Choose Student for independent exploration and guided investigations, or Teacher for classroom prompts and discussion. Search accepts exact atomic numbers, symbols, element names and recorded historical names. Group and family filters combine with the other controls. Group 3 selects Sc and Y in this layout; use Detached series for the lanthanoids and actinoids. Filters combine; unmatched elements remain faintly visible to preserve the table's structure. Opening any element is still possible.

Hidden names, numbers and masses remain hidden in the details until you choose Reveal identity. These controls support classroom recall, not secure assessment: historical prose and exported data may identify an element. CSV exports all 118 records and all three temperatures, regardless of filters. Shell columns are populated only for the first 20 neutral atoms; a blank beyond calcium means outside this model, not zero.

First 20 enlarges hydrogen to calcium while retaining labelled groups and periods; groups 3–12 are shown as an explicit gap. Select a tile to open its full information panel and update the shell explorer. The explorer’s Previous/Next buttons let you compare shells without opening a panel. Previous/Next steps through all first 20 elements, independently of filters. Predict and reveal hides shell populations in the explorer; table pattern colouring can still provide clues. Reset restores all 118 elements, Student mode, 25 °C, all filters and all labels. To share, copy this single HTML file; open it in a modern browser. Data and features work without internet access.
