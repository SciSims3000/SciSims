Pattern helper

Use these rules to fill the worksheet table without memorising every row.

  • Name ending: all of these hydrocarbons end in -ane because they are alkanes.
  • Carbon count: meth-, eth-, prop-, but-, pent-, hex-, hept-, oct- mean 1 to 8 carbon atoms.
  • Molecular formula: for an alkane, use CnH2n+2.
  • Condensed formula: ends are usually CH3; middle carbon groups are CH2.
  • Hydrogen toggle: switch hydrogens on to count H atoms separately, then switch them off for a cleaner condensed structural formula view.

How to use this activity

  1. Select an alkane and observe the number of carbon atoms.
  2. Use CnH2n+2 to predict the molecular formula.
  3. Use the end and middle groups to write the condensed structural formula.
  4. Move to Fill the table and check your worksheet answers.

Quick example

Butane has four carbon atoms. Using CnH2n+2, its molecular formula is C4H10. The two end groups are CH3, and the two middle groups are CH2, so the condensed structural formula is CH3CH2CH2CH3.

Teacher notes

  • Purpose: support students to complete an alkane formula table using patterns rather than copying answers.
  • Key idea: straight-chain alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons with only single covalent bonds and the molecular formula pattern CnH2n+2.
  • Common error: students often write CnH2n instead of CnH2n+2. Ask them to check the two extra hydrogens on the end carbons.
  • Common error: students may confuse molecular formula with condensed structural formula. The molecular formula gives atom totals; the condensed structural formula gives the order of carbon groups.
  • Scope note: this activity focuses on straight-chain alkanes from methane to octane. Branched isomers are not included.
