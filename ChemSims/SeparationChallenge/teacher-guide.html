Teacher guide · Years 7–10

Separation Challenge

Inspect a mixture, choose techniques and recover its components. Every fraction matters: decide what to keep and what to do next.

Suggested time: 20–35 minutes. Use a projected demonstration, pairs or individual investigation.

Quick start

  1. Choose a mission and read its recovery goal. Use Inspect physical properties before planning a sequence.
  2. Select a fraction, then apply a technique. Read the explanation and select the next fraction to process.
  3. Track the mass in containers and water lost to air. Use Undo to investigate a different sequence.
  4. Complete the recovery goal and export the pathway. Explain why each technique was suitable.

Discussion and expected reasoning

Why does filtration fail to separate dissolved salt from water?

Dissolved salt passes through ordinary filter paper with the water. Filtration retains insoluble particles.

How can salt and sand be separated, and how does the method change if water must also be recovered?

Add water and dissolve the salt, filter out sand, then evaporate the filtrate to retain salt. Use distillation instead of evaporation when the water must also be collected.

Which physical properties distinguish iron, gravel, sand and oil?

Iron is magnetic; gravel exceeds the sieve opening size; sand is insoluble; oil is immiscible with water and forms the upper layer.

Support and extension

Model one trial and ask students to make a prediction before operating the controls. Give students a results-table heading or sentence starter if needed. For extension, ask them to justify their method, evaluate an assumption and design a follow-up investigation.

Assess the explanation and evidence, not just completion of the activity.

Model limits

Ideal recovery: no transfer loss, residual moisture or incomplete separation. Filter residue is shown as dry; real residues may require washing and drying. All salt dissolves at the chosen water-to-salt ratio. Water is the only volatile solvent. Mixed oil fractions cannot be heated in this model. This is not practical apparatus guidance or a risk assessment.
