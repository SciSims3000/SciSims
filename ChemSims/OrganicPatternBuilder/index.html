Organic Pattern Builder

Build straight-chain organic molecules up to ten carbons, compare single, double and triple bonds, and identify common functional groups used in senior Chemistry.

Learning focus Organic formulae and names are built from a parent carbon chain, bond type, locants and functional groups. The same molecular formula can represent different structural isomers.

How to use this activity

  • Start with the main carbon chain and identify the parent prefix: meth, eth, prop, but, pent, hex, hept, oct, non or dec.
  • Change the bond type to compare saturated and unsaturated hydrocarbons.
  • Move the multiple bond or functional group to compare positional isomers.
  • Use the name breakdown to connect locants, prefixes and suffixes to the displayed structure.
  • Ask students to explain why some options disappear when the molecule would be invalid or chemically misleading at this level.

Teacher notes

  • Purpose: this is a pattern-recognition model, not a full organic nomenclature engine. It helps students connect parent chain length, bond saturation, functional groups, locants, formulae and condensed structural formulae.
  • Target course: upper high school and NSW Chemistry Module 7 Organic Chemistry. It is most useful before students attempt more complex structural formulae, isomerism and reaction pathways.
  • Core sequence: begin with alkanes to revise CnH2n+2, then compare alkenes and alkynes to show how multiple bonds reduce the hydrogen count.
  • Functional groups: introduce haloalkanes, alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, amines, amides and esters as patterns that alter both the name and formula.
  • Isomer focus: use but-1-ene/but-2-ene, propan-1-ol/propan-2-ol and 1-chloropropane/2-chloropropane to show that compounds can share a molecular formula but have different structures.
  • Common error: students often treat a displayed condensed formula as a molecular formula. Require them to count atoms from the structural formula before writing the molecular formula.
  • Common error: students often number from the wrong end of the chain. The model uses simplified locants, but teachers should still reinforce the lowest-number rule.
  • Limitation: this version uses straight chains only. It does not model branching, rings, aromatic compounds, stereoisomerism, E/Z isomerism, chirality or multiple functional groups on the same molecule.

Suggested lesson flow

  1. Students build methane to decane and state the alkane general formula.
  2. Students switch to alkenes and alkynes and explain the change in hydrogen count.
  3. Students move the bond or group position and identify possible positional isomers.
  4. Students compare one hydrocarbon, one alcohol, one carbonyl compound and one carboxylic acid.
  5. Students write a short explanation: “How does the structure determine the name and molecular formula?”
