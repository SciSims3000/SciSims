How the model works

What should I notice?

As the amount of gas increases, the piston rises and the gas occupies a larger volume. At fixed temperature and pressure, the ratio V/n stays constant.

Why does the piston move?

Adding gas would increase collision frequency and pressure if the container stayed rigid. The freely moving piston rises until the gas pressure returns to the external pressure.

What is idealised?

The model treats the gas as ideal and the piston as frictionless. The displayed particles are a scaled visual representation. Real gases can deviate from ideal behaviour, especially at high pressure or low temperature.

What does the slope mean?

The gradient of the V–n graph is the molar volume under the selected conditions: RT/P.

Teacher notes

  • Begin in Learn mode and ask students to compare 1.0 mol, 2.0 mol and 4.0 mol.
  • In Investigate mode, students can deliberately change temperature or pressure and identify why those trials are not a controlled Avogadro comparison.
  • The visual particle count is representative. The calculated entity count uses the exact SI value of the Avogadro constant.
  • Challenge mode retains its score when students move between modes or reopen the page.

Scientific basis and sources

The model calculates ideal-gas volume using PV = nRT, then displays the Avogadro relationship at controlled temperature and pressure.
