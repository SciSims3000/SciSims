How to use

  • Start the model and watch the neutron activity, heat transfer and turbine output.
  • Move the control rods in or out to control the fission chain reaction.
  • Increase coolant flow to remove heat from the core more effectively.
  • Use SCRAM to rapidly insert the rods and reduce the reaction.
  • Turn on Random events to practise responding to changing reactor conditions.
Challenge: Produce at least 60% electrical output while keeping the safety margin at Good or Stable. In Random events mode, explain what changed and how you responded.

What the model shows

A nuclear reactor uses a controlled chain reaction. Fission releases neutrons and heat. Control rods absorb some neutrons, coolant removes heat from the core, and a steam-turbine system converts heat energy into electrical energy.

Key idea: More fission does not automatically mean a better reactor. The useful goal is a controlled, stable reaction with enough cooling and a safe operating margin.
Component guide:
  • Fuel rods contain atoms that can undergo fission.
  • Control rods absorb neutrons and slow the chain reaction.
  • Coolant removes heat from the core.
  • Steam generator transfers heat to produce steam.
  • Turbine and generator convert moving steam into electrical energy.

This is a simplified teaching model. It shows energy transfer and feedback patterns, not a real reactor design or operating procedure.

Teacher notes

  • Use this to connect fission, chain reactions, heat transfer and electricity generation.
  • The model is deliberately conceptual: it uses arbitrary classroom units rather than real reactor operating data.
  • Good prompts: What happens when rods are inserted? What happens when cooling is reduced? Why is a stable reaction more useful than an uncontrolled one?
  • Emphasise that the simulator is about safe conceptual understanding, not engineering design.
