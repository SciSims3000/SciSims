Magnetic Field Around a Wire

Explore the magnetic field produced by a straight current-carrying wire. Change the current and wire position to see how the field direction and strength pattern change.

Physics Electricity Magnetism Right-hand rule

Learning Intention

Students will understand that a current-carrying wire produces a circular magnetic field around the wire.

Success Criteria

Students can use field arrows to describe magnetic field direction, explain the effect of current direction, and relate field strength to distance from the wire.

Student challenge

  • Set the current to +5 A. Describe the direction of the field arrows.
  • Change the current to −5 A. What changes?
  • Increase the current magnitude. What happens to the arrow length and colour?
  • Drag the probe closer to the wire. What happens to the relative field strength?
  • Explain why the field is strongest close to the wire.

Teacher notes

This model is designed for visualising the magnetic field around a long straight current-carrying wire. The square plotting area keeps distances equal in the x and y directions so that circular field lines are represented accurately.

  • Good prompt: How do we know the field is circular rather than straight?
  • Good prompt: What does reversing the current do to the field direction?
  • Extension: Link the model to the right-hand grip rule and the equation for field strength near a long straight wire.

Formula used in the model

The sim uses a simplified version of the magnetic field around a long straight wire. The real expression is:

B = μ₀I ÷ 2πr B = magnetic field strength μ₀ = permeability of free space I = current through the wire r = distance from the wire

In this sim, the exact physical constant is not needed because the arrows show relative field strength. The model keeps the key relationship: larger current gives a stronger field, and greater distance gives a weaker field.

How to explain it to students

  • Current direction matters: reversing the current reverses the magnetic field direction.
  • Distance matters: field strength decreases as you move away from the wire.
  • Right-hand grip rule: point your right thumb in the direction of conventional current; your curled fingers show the magnetic field direction.
  • Model limitation: the sim shows a flat cross-section of a 3D field around a straight wire.
