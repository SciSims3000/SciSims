Magnetic Field Around Two Wires

Explore how magnetic fields from two current-carrying wires combine. Change the current direction, current size and wire positions to see constructive and opposing field patterns.

Physics Electricity Magnetism Superposition

Learning Intention

Students will understand that the magnetic field at a point near two current-carrying wires is the vector sum of the field from each wire.

Success Criteria

Students can describe how same-direction and opposite-direction currents change the combined magnetic field pattern between and around two wires.

Teacher notes

This model focuses on vector superposition. Students should notice that fields can strengthen or partly cancel depending on current directions and the position being tested.

  • Good prompt: Where do the two fields point in the same direction?
  • Good prompt: Where do the two fields point in opposite directions?
  • Extension: Connect the model to the force between parallel current-carrying wires.

Formula used in the model

Each wire uses the same straight-wire field relationship. The combined field is found by adding the field vectors from both wires.

For one long straight wire: B = μ₀I ÷ 2πr For this simulation, relative field is shown as: Brelative = |I| ÷ r For two wires: Bresultant = B₁ + B₂ B₁ and B₂ are vectors, so direction matters. Fields can reinforce or partially cancel.

The exact physical constant is not needed because this sim displays relative field strength. It preserves the key relationship: stronger current gives a stronger field, and greater distance gives a weaker field.

How to explain it to students

  • Each wire creates a circular magnetic field around itself.
  • The fields add as vectors, so both size and direction matter.
  • Same-direction currents produce one type of combined pattern.
  • Opposite-direction currents produce a different pattern, especially between the wires.
  • Model limitation: this is a flat cross-section of a 3D magnetic field.
