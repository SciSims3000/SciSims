Light Intensity — Inverse Square Law

Investigate how irradiance changes with distance from a point source, collect repeated measurements and test the inverse-square model using linearisation and regression.

Learning intention

Explain how the irradiance from an isotropic point source changes with distance, and test the inverse-square relationship experimentally.

Success criteria

Collect repeated readings while controlling variables, interpret standard deviation, and use a graph of E against 1/r² to calculate source power and evaluate error.

Physics model
E = P / (4πr²)

Here, light intensity is represented by irradiance: radiant power received per unit area.

Lamp–sensor experiment

The same radiant power spreads over an area proportional to r².

Expected irradiance
7.96 W/m²
Why does intensity decrease?

An equal detector area captures a progressively smaller fraction of the emitted energy as the spherical wavefront expands.

Equal-area detector

Normalised so that 1.0 m corresponds to 36 relative packets. Doubling distance gives about one quarter as many.
