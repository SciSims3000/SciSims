CRO Sound Oscillator

A cathode ray oscilloscope, or CRO, displays a changing signal as a graph of signal strength against time. Use this sim to investigate how frequency, amplitude, phase and time scale change the trace of a sound signal.

Physics Waves Sound Oscilloscopes

Learning Intention

Students will understand how a CRO-style display can represent a sound wave as a changing signal over time.

Success Criteria

Students can describe frequency, amplitude, period and phase, and explain how each one changes the oscilloscope trace.

Student challenge

  • Set the frequency to 440 Hz. Record the period shown by the sim.
  • Double the frequency and describe what happens to the period.
  • Increase the amplitude and explain what changes on the trace.
  • Change the phase. What changes about the wave, and what stays the same?
  • Adjust the time scale until you can clearly see several wave cycles.

Teacher notes

This is a simplified CRO-style signal model. It represents how a sound wave can be displayed as a voltage-like signal over time. It does not show the physical shape of air particles moving through space.

  • Good prompt: Why does a higher frequency produce more cycles on the same screen?
  • Good prompt: Why does amplitude affect loudness but not pitch?
  • Extension: Ask students to calculate period from frequency and compare it with the readout.
