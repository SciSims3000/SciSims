Electroscope Induction

Separate charge without touching. Connect to Earth, choose the order of your actions, and discover when the electroscope keeps a charge.

Learning intention

Explain how a nearby charged object redistributes electrons, and how earthing can leave an electroscope charged by induction.

Success criteria

Distinguish polarisation from net charging, predict leaf behaviour, and explain why the earth connection must be removed before the inducing object.

Charge by induction

  1. Begin with a neutral, isolated electroscope.
  2. Bring a charged wand near without touching.
  3. Connect the electroscope to Earth.
  4. Disconnect Earth while the wand stays near.
  5. Remove the wand.

Interpret the evidence

Polarisation: electrons redistribute within the electroscope; its total charge need not change.

Earthing: electrons can enter or leave through a conducting path to Earth.

Induction: disconnecting Earth before removing the wand traps a net charge opposite to the wand.

Leaf separation: both leaves have the same sign and repel. Separation alone does not identify that sign.

Model and limitations

This is a qualitative electrostatic model. Leaf angles and charge symbols illustrate charge redistribution, not calibrated charge, voltage, force or a geometrically exact electric field. The conducting enclosure components shown are electrically connected; the support and outer enclosure are insulating.

The model divides induced imbalance between the terminal and two identical leaves. A nearby wand influences the terminal most strongly. Earthing cancels the model’s leaf imbalance while the wand remains nearby; real electroscopes can retain some field-dependent leaf deflection.

Contact charging uses a simplified equal-capacitance charge-sharing model for the wand and electroscope. While the wand touches an earthed electroscope, both discharge to Earth. The wand retreats slightly after contact. An insulating charged rod would not behave as this conducting wand does.

Blue arrows show the direction of electron transfer during the most recent earthing action; they do not indicate a continuing current. Positive charge means a deficit of electrons; it does not mean positive ions travel through the wire. At this schematic level, the positions of individual charge symbols are illustrative.
