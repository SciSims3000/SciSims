Model, method and limitations

The block is a non-rolling rigid object on a uniform ramp. Air resistance, surface irregularity and the difference between starting and stopping friction are not modelled. Coefficients are constant; gravity is 9.81 m/s².

Released from rest, the block holds when mg sin θ ≤ μₛmg cos θ. Equality is treated as limiting equilibrium. Above this threshold it slides with constant acceleration a = g(sin θ − μₖ cos θ). The critical angle is θc = arctan μₛ. Mass cancels in both predictions.

For each sliding run, the transit time is t = √(2L/a). Optional simulated timing variation is added to that time; the measured acceleration for each repeat is 2L/t². Recorded acceleration is the mean of these individual estimates, with sample standard deviation. No timing variation is added to the animation or the critical-angle tests.

The linear graph includes sliding results only. Its expected slope is 1 and intercept is −μₖ. Ordinary least squares gives each recorded angle equal weight. Standard-deviation error bars describe repeat spread, not uncertainty in the mean. Ideal repeats are identical when timing variation is zero.

Critical-angle observations give a bracket, not an exact measurement. If θlow holds and θhigh slides, μₛ lies from tan θlow up to tan θhigh (exclusive at the upper end).
