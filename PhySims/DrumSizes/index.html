Student challenge

  • Keep tension the same and increase drum diameter. What happens to the estimated pitch?
  • Keep diameter the same and increase tension. What happens to the estimated pitch?
  • Set both drums to equal size and equal tension. What happens?
  • Change damping. Does it mainly affect pitch or how long the sound lasts?
  • Explain why a large drum usually sounds lower than a small drum.

Teacher notes

This sim is useful for linking sound, frequency, pitch and vibration. It also works as a controlled-variable investigation: change one factor while keeping the others constant.

  • Good prompt: Which variable changes pitch more in this model: diameter or tension?
  • Good prompt: What is the difference between pitch and volume?
  • Extension: Ask students to collect pitch estimates and graph pitch against diameter.

Formula used in the model

The model estimates pitch using a simplified relationship between membrane tension and drum diameter.

Simplified model: estimated pitch ∝ √tension ÷ diameter In this sim: estimated pitch = 5200 × √tension ÷ diameter Where: pitch is measured in hertz tension is a relative multiplier diameter is measured in centimetres Main pattern: larger diameter = lower pitch greater tension = higher pitch greater damping = shorter sound

How to explain it to students

  • Pitch depends on vibration frequency.
  • Higher frequency means higher pitch.
  • Lower frequency means lower pitch.
  • Larger diameter usually lowers the pitch because the membrane vibrates more slowly.
  • Greater tension usually raises the pitch because the membrane vibrates faster.
  • Damping affects how quickly the vibration dies away.
