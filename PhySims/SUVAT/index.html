Student challenge

  • Use the given values of u, a and t to predict v and s before running the model.
  • Choose the equation you used for v and the equation you used for s.
  • After the run, compare your prediction with the actual result.
  • Explain whether your graph prediction matched the actual motion.
  • Try a braking case and predict whether the object changes direction.

Teacher notes

This version hides the answer until students make a prediction. It is best used as a Predict → Run → Compare → Explain activity rather than a calculator.

  • Good prompt: Which values are given before the run?
  • Good prompt: Which equation lets you calculate final velocity first?
  • Good prompt: Why might a prediction be close but not exact?

Formula used in the model

The model uses constant-acceleration equations.

SUVAT variables: s = displacement u = initial velocity v = final velocity a = acceleration t = time Prediction equations used most often here: v = u + at s = ut + ½at² The full SUVAT set: v = u + at s = ut + ½at² s = vt − ½at² v² = u² + 2as s = ½(u + v)t Graph links: gradient of displacement-time graph = velocity gradient of velocity-time graph = acceleration area under velocity-time graph = displacement

How to explain it to students

  • s means displacement, not speed.
  • u is the starting velocity.
  • v is the final velocity.
  • a is constant acceleration.
  • t is the time interval.
  • Students should predict first, then compare their prediction with the model result.
