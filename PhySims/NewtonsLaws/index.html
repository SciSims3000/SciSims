Main idea

First law

If net force is zero, velocity stays constant. Objects do not need a continuing force to keep moving.

Second law

Acceleration depends on net force and mass: a = Fnet ÷ m. Bigger force gives bigger acceleration.

Third law

Interactions come in equal-sized, opposite-direction force pairs acting on different objects.

Student challenge

  • Watch each explainer animation and identify the force evidence.
  • In prediction mode, set friction to zero and give the cart an initial velocity. What happens when the net force is zero?
  • Keep force the same and increase mass. What happens to acceleration?
  • In third-law mode, make one cart heavier. Which cart has the bigger acceleration?
  • Explain why equal and opposite forces do not cancel if they act on different objects.

Teacher notes

This sim begins with explicit instruction before moving to prediction. Use Explainer mode for modelling, then Prediction mode for student reasoning.

  • First law prompt: Does a moving object need a continuing force?
  • Second law prompt: What changes acceleration more: force or mass?
  • Third law prompt: If the forces are equal, why can the accelerations be different?

Formula used in the model

The model uses straight-line force and motion relationships.

First law: If Fnet = 0, velocity stays constant. Second law: Fnet = ma a = Fnet ÷ m Third law: Force of A on B = − Force of B on A Important distinction: The forces in a third-law pair are equal in size, but they act on different objects. They do not cancel each other on one object.

How to explain it to students

  • Force is a push or pull.
  • Net force is the total force on one object.
  • Acceleration means a change in velocity.
  • Mass resists changes in motion.
  • Third-law pairs act on different objects, so they do not cancel each other.
