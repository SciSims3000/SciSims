Teacher guide · Years 7–10

Heat Transfer House

Change insulation, glazing and ventilation. Compare controlled trials and follow the energy moving into and out of a model home.

Suggested time: 25–40 minutes. Use a projected demonstration, pairs or individual investigation.

Quick start

  1. Select a weather preset. Keep outdoor temperature, sunlight and starting indoor temperature fixed for a controlled comparison.
  2. Choose a house design and select Set as baseline A. Change just one variable, such as wall and roof R-value.
  3. Use the time slider and both curves to compare the designs. Read the one-variable comparison message.
  4. Select Record design B for each design worth keeping. Export CSV for all recorded temperatures and settings.

Discussion and expected reasoning

What happens when insulation increases on a cold, sunless day?

Greater R-value reduces envelope heat transfer, so the indoor temperature approaches the colder outdoor temperature more slowly.

Does opening windows always cool a house?

No. Ventilation moves indoor temperature towards outdoor temperature; if outside is hotter, increased ventilation increases heat gain.

Why might insulation keep a house warmer during a cool evening after a hot day?

Insulation slows heat loss as well as heat gain. If the house starts hotter than the outside air, insulation can retain that heat.

Support and extension

Model one trial and ask students to make a prediction before operating the controls. Give students a results-table heading or sentence starter if needed. For extension, ask them to justify their method, evaluate an assumption and design a follow-up investigation.

Assess the explanation and evidence, not just completion of the activity.

Save and share

Results, settings and notes save in this browser when storage is available. Export JSON backup preserves the whole lab state; importing replaces the current work after confirmation. Export results CSV provides recorded data or the separation pathway. Print / Save as PDF captures the displayed investigation and notes.

Browser data is not synchronised between devices. Export before clearing browser data. The notes field supports 30 000 characters; the app states any result limits.

Model limits

One indoor temperature, fixed weather and no heater or air conditioner. Wall area 120 m²; roof 80 m²; windows 12 m²; volume 200 m³. Effective R/U values combine envelope conduction and surface effects. Solar transmittance is controlled separately from glazing U-value. Floor transfer, occupants, moisture and separate long-wave surface radiation are omitted. Parameters are illustrative; this is not a building-rating tool.
