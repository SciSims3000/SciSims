Sound Wave Generator

Generate simple tones and compare sine, square, triangle and sawtooth waves. Use the oscilloscope display to connect what you hear with the shape of the signal.

Physics Sound Waves Frequency

Learning Intention

Students will understand that different sound waves can have different frequencies, amplitudes and waveform shapes.

Success Criteria

Students can change pitch, volume and waveform type, then relate what they hear to the signal shown on the scope.

Sound safety note

Keep device volume low before starting the sound. Some waveforms can sound harsher than a sine wave even at the same frequency.

Waveform display

The scope shows the shape of the signal being generated. A sine wave is smooth, while square and sawtooth waves change more sharply.

Student challenge

  • Start with a 440 Hz sine wave. Describe the sound and the shape of the trace.
  • Double the frequency to 880 Hz. What changes about the pitch?
  • Change the waveform to square. What changes about the sound and trace?
  • Lower the relative volume. What changes on the screen?
  • Compare sine, triangle, square and sawtooth waves at the same frequency.

Teacher notes

This sim is useful for connecting pitch, loudness and waveform shape. It also helps students see that two tones can have the same frequency but different waveform shapes and therefore different sound qualities.

  • Good prompt: Which control changes pitch?
  • Good prompt: Why can two waves have the same frequency but sound different?
  • Extension: Introduce timbre as a difference in waveform and harmonic content.
