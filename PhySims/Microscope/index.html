How to use

  • Start with the 4× or 10× objective to find the specimen.
  • Use stage controls to centre the specimen before increasing magnification.
  • Each new view begins very blurred and off centre, so you need to move the stage and focus it yourself.
  • Use coarse focus for large adjustments on low power.
  • Use fine focus when the image is nearly sharp or when using high power.
  • Increase light intensity and adjust the diaphragm if the image is too dark or washed out.
Challenge: Find the onion cells on low power, centre them, then switch to 400× and get the image clear using fine focus.

What the model shows

A compound light microscope uses an objective lens near the specimen and an eyepiece lens near the eye. Total magnification is found by multiplying the objective magnification by the eyepiece magnification.

At higher magnification, the field of view and depth of field become smaller. This means less of the specimen is visible and focus becomes more sensitive.

Teacher notes

  • Use this before a practical to practise microscope handling decisions.
  • Emphasise that high magnification is not always better: brightness, field of view, centring and focus all matter.
  • The microscope view is a simplified model designed to support conceptual understanding rather than exact optical calculation.
