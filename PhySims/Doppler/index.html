Doppler Effect Simulator

Visualise how waves are compressed or stretched when a source moves relative to an observer.

Learning Intention

Students will understand that the Doppler effect occurs when relative motion changes the frequency observed from a wave source.

Success Criteria

Students can describe how source motion affects wavelength and observed frequency, compare approaching and receding motion, and use data to explain a Doppler shift.

Controls

Approaching: f observed = f × v ÷ (v − v source)
Receding: f observed = f × v ÷ (v + v source)
This is a scaled classroom model. The visual waves show the pattern clearly, but they do not represent a real siren or ripple tank exactly.

Student challenge

Record three trials where the source moves towards the observer and three trials where it moves away. Keep the source frequency the same each time. Explain how changing the source speed changes the observed frequency and wavelength.

Teacher notes

This simulation shows the classical Doppler effect for a moving source and a stationary observer. It is suitable for modelling sound waves, water waves or other waves where the source moves through a medium. It does not model relativistic Doppler shift for light.

Assessing the simulation: Students should identify that the sim helps visualise wavefront compression and spreading. They should also recognise limitations, including the simplified one-dimensional observer model, scaled visual speeds, no reflection or interference, and no shock wave or sonic-boom behaviour.
