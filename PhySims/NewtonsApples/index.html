Newton's Angry Apples

Launch apples, test variables and use physics evidence to solve a silly projectile-motion puzzle.

Learning Intention

Students will understand that projectile motion is affected by launch angle, launch power, gravity, mass, air resistance and collisions.

Success Criteria

Students can change one variable at a time, collect launch data, compare results and explain how forces and energy affected the motion of the apple.

Student Challenge

  1. Use Mission 1 to find the launch angle that gives the greatest modelled distance on Earth with a Regular Apple.
  2. Change only the apple type. Which apple transfers the most energy to the blocks? Use evidence from your trials.
  3. Compare Earth and Moon gravity. How does lower gravity change the path of the projectile?
  4. Use Mission 4 to increase air resistance. What happens to modelled distance and highest impact speed?
  5. Write one conclusion using the structure: “When ___ increased, ___ changed because ___.”

Teacher Notes

  • This is a simplified model, not a precise physics engine.
  • Good variables for fair testing: angle, launch power, gravity, air resistance, apple type and wind.
  • Useful discussion terms: force, acceleration, gravity, mass, inertia, kinetic energy, momentum, friction and air resistance.
  • For a data task, ask students to run at least five repeated trials while changing only one variable.
  • Limitations include simplified drag, simplified collision behaviour and stylised scoring.
  • Version note: launch power and air resistance have been tuned for classroom playability rather than strict numerical realism.
