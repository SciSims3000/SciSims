Longitudinal Wave Simulation

Investigate how amplitude, wavelength, frequency and wave speed affect a longitudinal wave. Watch particles vibrate back and forth while compressions and rarefactions move through the medium.

Physics Waves Longitudinal waves Sound models

Learning Intention

Students will understand that a longitudinal wave transfers energy through compressions and rarefactions while particles vibrate parallel to the direction of wave travel.

Success Criteria

Students can identify compressions, rarefactions, wavelength and amplitude, and explain how particle motion differs from transverse waves.

Student challenge

  • Run the wave and describe how each particle moves.
  • Pause the wave and identify one compression and one rarefaction.
  • Increase the amplitude. What changes about the particles?
  • Increase the wavelength. What happens to the spacing between compressions?
  • Compare this with the transverse wave sim. What is the main difference in particle motion?

Teacher notes

This sim is useful for showing that longitudinal waves involve particle vibration parallel to the direction of wave travel. It is especially helpful before introducing sound as a pressure wave.

  • Good prompt: What evidence shows this is a longitudinal wave?
  • Good prompt: Do the particles travel with the wave, or vibrate around fixed positions?
  • Extension: Ask students to explain why sound cannot be drawn accurately as a transverse wave.
