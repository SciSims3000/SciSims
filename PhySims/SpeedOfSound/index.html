Speed of Sound — Flag & Horn

See the flag. Hear the horn. Measure the delay and use repeated trials to find how quickly sound travels through air.

Learning intention

Explain the delay between seeing and hearing a simultaneous signal, and determine the speed of sound experimentally.

Success criteria

Collect repeated times at several distances, use a best-fit graph to estimate speed, and distinguish random timing variation from systematic bias.

Method, model and limitations

This is a simulated field-timing investigation, not a test of your reaction time. A trial starts when the simultaneous source signals are sent. The visual delay is treated as zero: at 1 km, light travel time is only about 3.3 microseconds, negligible beside sound travel time.

In ideal mode, the recorded delay is d/v. In field mode, the delay is d/v + b + ε, where b is the selected systematic timing bias and ε is a new uniformly distributed error within the selected ± variation for every trial. Control limits keep all measured delays positive. The animation shows the physical sound travel time; the displayed measured time may differ because of timing error.

The graph uses ordinary least squares on recorded means with a free intercept. All distances receive equal weight. Error bars show sample standard deviation of repeated times, not uncertainty in the mean. A slope of m gives v = 1/m. Identical distances replace the previous recorded mean. Changing distance clears unfinished repeats; recorded results lock temperature and timing conditions.

The optional audio cue is for illustration only. Browser audio latency is not included in measurements. Pausing, slow playback and tab switching do not alter the recorded physical travel time.
