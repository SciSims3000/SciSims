Reflection and Refraction Law Simulator

Investigate the law of reflection, Snell’s law and total internal reflection by changing angles, materials and measurement uncertainty.

Learning Intention

Students will understand that the path of a light ray can be predicted using the law of reflection and the law of refraction.

Success Criteria

Students can measure angles, compare incident, reflected and refracted rays, apply Snell’s law, and identify when total internal reflection occurs.

Law focus

Reasoning chain

Measure angles → Compare the relationship → Apply the law.

Reflection

The incident ray and reflected ray are measured from the normal. In reflection, these angles are equal.

Refraction

A ray bends when it crosses into a material with a different refractive index. Snell’s law predicts the refracted angle.

Total internal reflection

When light travels from a higher refractive index to a lower refractive index, angles above the critical angle produce total internal reflection.

Student challenge

  1. Use reflection mode. Record trials at 10°, 30°, 50° and 70°. Describe the relationship between incident and reflected angles.
  2. Use refraction mode from air into glass. Describe whether the ray bends towards or away from the normal.
  3. Compare air to water, air to glass and air to diamond. Explain how refractive index affects bending.
  4. Use total internal reflection mode. Increase the incident angle above the critical angle for glass to air, then compare it with water to air.
  5. Evaluate the simulation: what does it help explain, what does it simplify, and how is it different from a real ray box practical?

CER response

Teacher notes

  • Suggested primary module: Investigating Science Module 6, Technologies, if used as a ray-box or optical measurement technology activity. Also suitable for Physics or Stage 4/5 light content.
  • This sim is law-focused rather than evidence-history focused.
  • Reflection uses θᵢ = θᵣ. Refraction uses n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂.
  • Total internal reflection occurs only when light travels from a higher refractive index into a lower refractive index and the incident angle exceeds the critical angle.
  • The simulation is simplified. It does not model ray thickness, partial reflection/refraction, polarisation, wavelength dispersion or real measurement error.
  • Students should assess the simulation itself by discussing strengths, limitations, differences from a real practical, possible sources of error and overall usefulness.
