Advanced historical distinction

The full sequence of Venus’s phases challenged the traditional Ptolemaic arrangement, in which Venus stays between Earth and the Sun. It did not alone establish that Earth moves: Tycho Brahe’s geoheliocentric model also places Venus in orbit around the Sun while Earth remains stationary.

Model limitations and scientific choices

These are educational models, not historical numerical ephemerides. The simple geocentric model uses uniform Earth-centred circles; its daily Sun motion can fit a familiar observation but its planets have no retrograde loops. Historical geocentric astronomy was much more capable than this simple model.

The Ptolemaic reconstruction sums two rotating vectors. Its adjustable deferent, epicycle and relative speeds generate illustrative retrograde loops, without reproducing every historical device or accurate dates. Matching a loop alone cannot distinguish geocentric and heliocentric explanations.

The early heliocentric model uses uniform circles. The modern model solves Kepler’s equation for fixed ellipses with the Sun at one focus; speed varies along each ellipse. Real dynamics includes mutual gravity and motion about the Solar System’s centre of mass. Gravity arrows illustrate attraction only and do not drive an N-body calculation.

All orbits are coplanar, fixed in orientation, with illustrative starting angles; calendar dates, inclinations, precession, light travel time and perturbations are omitted. Orbit distances share a linear AU scale within each view, but body sizes are enlarged. The Moon’s displayed Earth-distance is also exaggerated and its distance label is omitted. The Earth sky shows ecliptic longitude, not a local horizon. Daily Sun motion is a separate horizon diagram; one day is one illustrative rotation. Speed arrows apply to heliocentric views; gravity arrows are available in the modern model. Moon periods are approximate and deterministic, with simplified edge-on projections.

Traditional Venus geometry uses a small epicycle whose centre follows the Earth–Sun line and stays inside the Sun’s distance. The comparison is qualitative. Sun and Earth frames translate the viewpoint without changing model dynamics. The overview uses the natural centre of each model. The selected model determines the Venus geometry: A and B use the traditional inner arrangement; C and D use Sun-orbiting Venus. Venus uses circular orbits in this comparison, including in model D, to isolate the phase geometry.

Teacher user guide

Learning goal: compare historical and current models and explain how evidence leads scientists to modify or reject models.

  1. Start with Mars in the early heliocentric model. Pause, predict its next apparent motion, then advance in five-day steps. Day 0 begins near opposition; explore −100 to +100 days for the retrograde interval.
  2. Use the two panels together: the sightline determines the longitude on the fixed star field. “What is actually moving?” explains the distinction between orbital motion and apparent motion.
  3. Compare simple geocentric and Ptolemaic models. Adjust epicycles and discuss why reproducing one observation does not uniquely establish the physical arrangement.
  4. Choose Venus phases; drag Venus around its orbit and compare the two geometries. Open the advanced note before discussing Earth’s motion.
  5. Choose Jupiter’s moons. Advance successive nights and record positions, then enable the labelled orbital explanation.
  6. In Investigate mode, write predictions in the Evidence Laboratory. Use each test button, run the simulation, use “Record findings” to return directly to your result field. Export CSV before restarting. Entries save locally when the browser permits.
  7. Challenge mode has eight written responses. Feedback stays hidden until a nonempty response is committed. Teacher mode widens the display and hides challenge feedback; labels can be toggled.

Scientific reference checks: NASA: Mars retrograde motion; Museo Galileo: Venus phases; MIT: Venus phases and the Tychonic model. These optional references require internet; the simulation does not.

Keyboard: Space plays/pauses; left/right arrows step five days (1.2 hours in the daily activity) when focus is outside a form control. Every control also supports touch and standard keyboard navigation. On small screens, swipe within a diagram to read its labels at full size or open a large snapshot. Reference observation cards describe real observed patterns; the simulation supplies model predictions, not independent measurements. Reset time preserves records; Restart Investigation clears all responses and restores defaults after confirmation.
