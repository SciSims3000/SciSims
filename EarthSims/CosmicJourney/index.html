Teacher guide · Stage 4–6

Learning intention: Explain how the Universe changed over time and distinguish observations, evidence-based inferences and theoretical proposals.

Suggested sequence: (1) Ask students why a straight-line timeline would hide the early Universe. (2) Use Journey mode to narrate milestones. (3) Ask students to predict when atoms and stars could first form. (4) Return in Explore mode for discussion and challenge questions. (5) Compare observed evidence with predictions from models.

Critical misconceptions: The Big Bang was not an explosion into empty space; the Universe does not have a known centre of expansion. Earlier-than-CMB descriptions rely on physical theory, not direct images. Inflation is a significant but not conclusively established explanation. First-star timing is uncertain. The temperature shown for later eras is the cosmic background radiation temperature, not the temperature of stars or galaxies.

Classroom prompts: Why did neutral atoms form after nuclei? Why can we see the cosmic microwave background but not earlier photons directly? What observations could force a change in our current models?

Controls: Full screen fills the display and enables Focus view; Escape or Exit full screen returns to the previous layout. If the host browser blocks native full screen, an expanded panel view is offered with an explanatory message. Begin journey starts the presentation from blackness. Focus view enlarges the canvas and hides the side panels; Exit Focus view restores them. The colour legend and Labels & scale are optional; essential scientific caveats remain visible. Evidence badges explain observations, reconstructions and uncertainties; the highlighted badge indicates the emphasis of the selected milestone, not that the other categories are irrelevant. Timeline circles select milestones; early milestones share a group on the linear scale. Use previous/next buttons, select milestone cards, drag the timeline slider or press ←/→ while the simulator (rather than a form control) has keyboard focus. Journey playback pauses automatically at the end. Play journey starts automatic travel; its Pause freezes both motion and travel, and Resume continues from the same point. Pause animation freezes the current scene and also suspends any active journey; Resume animation restores the prior travel state. Explore mode allows manual scene selection and animation pause, with automatic journey playback disabled. Try this question again resets the current question. The opening uses one broad warm flare and a rapidly stretching field; it has no central blast, shell or repeated flashing. The flare is an artistic transition, not a prediction of inflation or a directly observed event. Reduced motion skips the flare and moving field. Reduced motion follows the system preference and can also be selected above: scenes change instantly and remain static while journey playback still advances. Restart restores complete blackness. Animation timing is for teaching, not a physical time scale. Switch between logarithmic and linear timeline scales to compare equal time ratios with equal time intervals. Milestone cards remain available when early events bunch together. Displayed milestone times are representative of broad eras. The expansion readout integrates a simplified Friedmann model, showing distance scale a and the volume a³ of a fixed comoving patch, not the size of the whole Universe. It does not drive the illustrative animations. Backward navigation shows the selected era already formed, rather than reversing physical processes. The nuclei sample contains 36 hydrogen nuclei and 3 helium-4 nuclei: approximately 75% hydrogen and 25% helium by mass, with trace isotopes omitted. The optional expansion grid represents changing distances, not a material lattice. Within-scene transformations illustrate cooling, binding and gravitational collapse; crossfades connect eras. Neither reconstructs the intervening physics quantitatively. Particle and atom panels use microscopic scales; gas and star panels use cosmic scales. Colours are illustrative, and electron paths are schematic.
