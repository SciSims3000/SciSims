How to use

  • Set the scenario, then manage the water system week by week.
  • Before each week, adjust restrictions, recycling/desalination, planned release and, in advanced mode, environmental flow and irrigation.
  • Spring in Sydney is usually a useful stress test because rainfall can be lower and demand begins to rise.
  • Watch for coastal rain events that can rapidly increase inflow and spill risk.
Challenge: Finish the full simulation with storage above 35%, average demand reliability above 85%, environmental health at Stable or Healthy, and flood spill under control.

What the model shows

This is a simplified Sydney-region catchment model. Sydney does not have one single dry season, but rainfall and demand are seasonal. The model uses lower spring rainfall pressure, higher summer demand and evaporation, occasional heavy coastal rain events, and optional long-term climate and demand trends.

Key idea: Water management is adaptive and cumulative. Short-term decisions can solve one week’s problem, but repeated choices affect long-term reliability, river health, cost and flood resilience.

Self-test

Teacher notes

  • Students can run the same starting scenario with different week-by-week decisions and compare long-term outcomes.
  • The climate is Sydney-style and conceptual: it is not a live forecast or a real dam operating model.
  • Good assessment task: students export CSV data, identify three decision points across multiple years, and justify how they changed their management plan.
  • Extension: ask students to compare a dry year, typical year and wet coastal year.
