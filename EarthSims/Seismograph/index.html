How to use

  • Start the earthquake and watch the seismogram trace build across the paper.
  • Identify the first small movement as the P-wave arrival.
  • Identify the stronger later shaking as the S-wave arrival.
  • Compare nearby and distant earthquakes to see how the S–P interval changes.
  • Turn on Hide distance and estimate the epicentre distance from the seismogram.

What the model shows

A seismograph records ground motion. During an earthquake, P-waves usually arrive first, followed by S-waves and then larger surface waves. The time between the P and S arrivals can be used to estimate how far the earthquake was from the station.

This model uses simplified wave speeds and amplitude scaling. Real seismologists use multiple stations, detailed travel-time models and more than one component of ground motion.

Teacher notes

  • Use this to introduce seismograms, P/S arrival times and epicentre distance.
  • Students should not treat the distance estimate as exact; it is a simplified model designed for pattern recognition.
  • Good follow-up: compare recordings from three stations and triangulate an epicentre.
